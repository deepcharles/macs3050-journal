### Question 2: Research question

The research question is whether perceived humanness and empathy are dissociated in relationship advice generated by a large language model, and how incentives to convey humanness and task characteristics shape perceptions of textual content.

It matters because differentiating generated and human-written content is becoming increasingly difficult, and the research showed that perceived humanness depends on incentives to appear human and the context in which writing is produced.

### Question 3: Data

The article states that 5 studies were conducted. In study 1, 587 participants were recruited via Prolific. They were randomly assigned to the adversarial group (n=315) or the naive condition group (n=272), 57 participants failed the manipulation check and were excluded.

All participants performed a writing task. Different details about fictious relationships were given in the task, resulting in 8 stimulus sets. Participants wrote relationship advice (min. 500 characters). Same task was done by GPT-4 which was prompted with identical questions as the humans.

### Question 4: Results

The study conducted that LLMs reportedly generate text indistinguishable from humans. Another finding was that LLMs, but not humans, adjusted their writing noticeably when instructed to be particularly human.

The data from the studies supports the findings. Study 1 summary stated "when instructed to be as human as possible, the LLM could drastically increase the humanness perception (+39.3%) while humans could not (+2.6%).
