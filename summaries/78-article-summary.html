Question 2: Research question

Does greater video-gaming experience predict better multitasking performance, and is this relationship explained by greater cognitive capacity?

Video gaming has been associated with better attention and information processing, but it is still unclear why gamers may perform better on complex multitasking tasks. The study tries to understand the mechanism behind this relationship.

Question 3: Data

  • Who / Cases: 59 participants were analyzed, aged 18–29 years with an average age of about 23. There were 45 men and 14 women, and most were military officer cadets.

  • What / Variables:

    • Video-gaming experience: weekly gaming time, previous gaming experience, and self-rated gaming expertise.
    • Multitasking performance: performance on system monitoring, tracking, communication, and resource-management tasks.
    • Cognitive capacity: information-processing speed and short-term/working-memory capacity.
    • Other variables included age, gender, military experience, media multitasking, and flight experience.

  • When: Data were collected from October to December 2023.

  • Where: The testing was conducted in a university laboratory, primarily associated with Universität der Bundeswehr München in Germany.

  • How was the data collected? Participants completed:

    • A visual short-term-memory test.
    • The openMATB multitasking test.
    • Questionnaires about demographics, media multitasking, and video-gaming experience.

    Gaming experience was mostly self-reported, while cognitive capacity and multitasking performance were measured experimentally using computer tasks.

Question 4: Results

  • Main results:

    • Greater video-gaming experience was associated with better multitasking performance.
    • Cognitive capacity did not significantly explain the relationship between gaming and multitasking.

  • Conclusion: Gaming experience may predict better multitasking performance, but the study could not clearly determine why this relationship exists.

  • Do the data support the conclusions? The data support the finding that gaming experience predicts better multitasking performance. However, they did not support the hypothesis that cognitive capacity explains this relationship. The relatively small sample size is also a limitation.

Question 6: Questions for the authors

Do you think the relationship between gaming experience and multitasking would be the same in a larger and more diverse sample?

Why do you think cognitive capacity did not significantly explain the relationship between gaming experience and multitasking performance?

Do you think certain types of video games, such as action or strategy games, improve multitasking more than others?
